Clackmannanshire council approves riverside solar farm

By Morag Finlay, Local Democracy Reporter. Published 12 October 2026.

Councillors in Clackmannanshire have approved plans for a 38-megawatt solar farm on farmland beside the River Devon, after a planning committee vote of seven to two on Thursday evening.

The scheme, proposed by Ochil Renewables Ltd, will cover about 62 hectares east of Tillicoultry. The developer says it will generate enough electricity for roughly 11,000 homes a year and will include a 20-megawatt battery to store power for evening peaks.

Construction is expected to begin in spring 2027 and take around nine months. The company has committed to a community benefit fund of £190,000 a year for the 40-year life of the site, to be managed by local community councils.

Objections over flooding and views

The application drew 143 objections, most concerning the visual impact on the Ochil Hills and the risk of flooding on the low-lying fields. Two councillors voted against, arguing that the drainage assessment relied on data that was more than a decade old.

SEPA, the Scottish Environment Protection Agency, withdrew its initial objection in August after the developer moved panels out of the 1-in-200-year flood zone and agreed to raise the battery compound by 1.2 metres.

What happens next

Because the farm is under 50 megawatts, the decision rests with the council rather than Scottish ministers. Objectors have 28 days to ask for a judicial review, although campaigners said on Friday they had not yet decided whether to do so.

The planning officer's report recommended approval subject to 24 conditions, including hedgerow planting along the A91 and a ban on construction traffic during school drop-off and pick-up times.
